How to Start an Online Business With a $0 Budget

You don't need savings to start selling online. You need one clear offer, one free place to show it, and a way to get paid. Here is the order that wastes the least time.

1. Pick something you can deliver this week

The fastest first income comes from things that cost nothing to produce: a service you can do (editing, design, bookkeeping help, tutoring), or a digital file you make once (a template, a checklist, a short guide). Physical products come later, once you know what people buy.

2. Check demand before you build

  • Search your idea on Etsy, Gumroad or Amazon and look at review counts. Reviews mean real buyers.
  • Search Reddit and Facebook groups for people asking for exactly this.
  • Use Google's autocomplete: type your idea and note what people add after it.

3. Use free tools only

  • Storefront: Gumroad and Payhip take a fee per sale and nothing upfront. Shopify has a free trial when you are ready for a full store.
  • Website: GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages host a simple site free.
  • Design: Canva's free plan and Google Fonts (free for commercial use).
  • Email: a free email-list plan from any major provider, for your first few hundred subscribers.

4. Make your first sale before you polish

Share the offer where your buyers already are. Answer questions honestly in communities, and follow each group's self-promotion rules. Ten conversations beat a perfect logo.

5. Register properly

Once money comes in, register as a business where you live, keep every receipt and talk to an accountant. In Israel, many beginners start as an osek patur (exempt dealer).

When to move to a real store

When you have repeat buyers or more than a few products, a dedicated store pays off. Shopify's free trial lets you build and test a store before paying anything.
